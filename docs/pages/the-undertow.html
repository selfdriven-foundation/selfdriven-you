When obsession turns destructive

The undertow.
When it starts to cost.

Destructive obsession rarely feels like drowning. It feels like swimming harder — while your health, money and the people you love drift further away. This page is for noticing the pull, counting the cost honestly, and getting back to shore.

03 · Red flags

Stop here if any of these are true.

These aren’t things to work through with a checklist. Each one is a reason to talk to a real person — today.

You’re thinking about hurting yourself

Or that people would be better off without you. You don’t have to be sure it’s “bad enough” to call.

Lifeline 13 11 14Suicide Call Back Service 1300 659 467Immediate danger 000

It’s about a person who has asked for space

You’re still checking on them, contacting them, or turning up — after they’ve pulled away or said stop. Whatever it feels like from inside, that’s harm to them. Stop contact now and talk to your GP or a psychologist. If it’s a partner or ex and you’re monitoring, controlling or frightening them, call the Men’s Referral Service.

No to Violence — Men’s Referral Service 1300 766 491

You’re hiding losses, or chasing money back

Debt you haven’t told anyone about. Borrowing to keep going. Betting to win back what’s gone. Tell someone tonight.

Gambling Help 1800 858 858

You’re using something to keep going — or to come down

Alcohol, stimulants or other drugs have become how you fuel it or how you switch it off, and it’s taking more.

National Alcohol and Other Drug Hotline 1800 250 015

The obsession is food, weight, your body or exercise

It’s taking over your days, your mood or your health. This is common, serious and treatable — and specialists know it well.

Butterfly Foundation 1800 33 4673
04 · The undertow’s voice

Why it’s so hard to see from inside.

Destructive obsession protects itself. It sounds reasonable, and it sounds like you. Learn the four voices, and the mode that answers each one.

Narrowing
“Nothing else really interests me anyway.”

The world shrinks until the obsession looks normal-sized. Without anything to compare it to, the cost becomes invisible.

Curiouswiden the frame
Hiding
“They wouldn’t understand.”

Secrecy feels like privacy. It’s actually the undertow cutting off the feedback — the people who would notice and say something.

Caringlet someone in
Escalating
“Just this once more.”

Each round takes a bit more to feel the same, and each “last time” resets the clock. The pull gets stronger by being obeyed.

Chilltake a real pause
Justifying
“I’ve put too much in to stop now.”

Sunk cost and “I’m so close” keep you going long after the evidence says stop. Ask when you first said you were close.

05 · Back to shore

The 4Cs, as a repair sequence.

Here the order matters. Chill creates the distance, Caring breaks the secrecy, Curious finds out what the obsession is doing for you, and Constructive builds the guardrails. Start at the top.

Willpower is the weakest tool here. Distance, people and structure do the heavy lifting — and professional help isn’t a last resort, it’s part of the sequence.

07 · Someone you love

If you’re watching someone get pulled under.

It’s its own kind of hard. The aim is caring without carrying all of it.

Caring, without carrying

  • Name what you see, specifically and kindly. The behaviour, not their character — “you’ve been up past 3 most nights this month” lands better than “you’re obsessed”.
  • Don’t cover for it. Paying debts, making excuses or keeping the secret removes the feedback that might help them stop.
  • Expect defensiveness. Say it once, clearly. Leave the door open. Ask what would help.
  • Look after yourself too. Most of the services below support family and friends as well — you can call them about someone else.

If you feel unsafe

If you’re frightened of them, being controlled, monitored or followed — that isn’t yours to manage alone, and it isn’t your fault.
