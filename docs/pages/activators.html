The role

What an activator actually does

No quotas, no scripts, no pressure. You're a facilitator: you bring the four-modes language into the places you already are, and guide people to the tools that help them notice where they're at.

Open the conversation

Introduce the four modes to your circle — classmates, teammates, family. Sometimes that's a workshop; mostly it's just asking "where you at?" and meaning it.

Model the practice

Name your own mode out loud. "I'm Chill at the moment," or "feeling Constructive today." When you do it, you make it safe for everyone else to.

Facilitate, don't fix

You're a guide, not a guru — your job is to hand people the practice, not to carry it for them. Show them the selfdriven.you tools, including the selfdriven.you skill that brings the four modes into their own AI assistant, so they can self-drive.

What you get

A community of peers

Good to know

Questions people ask

Do I need any qualifications? +
No. The whole point is that this is peer-to-peer. We give you the training and the kit — you bring your willingness to look out for people.
Am I expected to give mental-health advice? +
Never. Activators listen well and signpost to proper support. You're a friendly first point of contact, not a clinician — and the training makes that line very clear.
How much time does it take? +
As little as an hour or two a month. There are no quotas. You fit it around your life, and you can step back whenever you need to.
Is there an age limit? +
It's a mixed-age program. Younger activators operate within their school or club's safeguarding arrangements; everyone gets the same induction and support.
What's this "credential you own" thing? +
Your activator status is issued as a credential you hold yourself — not a line on someone else's database. It's portable, tamper-evident proof of what you've contributed that stays yours for life.
