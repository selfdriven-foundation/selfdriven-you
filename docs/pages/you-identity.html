Three ideas to understand first

AID — Autonomous Identifier
Your self-certifying ID
An AID is created from your own cryptographic key pair. No third party issues it. No one can revoke it except you. It's provably yours because only you hold the private key.
KEL — Key Event Log
Your tamper-proof history
Every action on your identity — creation, key rotation, delegation — is recorded as a signed event in an append-only log. It can be verified by anyone, trusted by everyone.
ACDC — Verifiable Credential
Claims anchored to your AID
Once your identity is established, organisations can issue credentials to your AID — professional qualifications, memberships, verifications — that only you can present.

Step 03 / 04
Secure your key with a passkey

Your Ed25519 private key will be encrypted using a secret derived from your passkey (WebAuthn PRF extension) and stored in your browser. Only your biometric can unlock it. The raw seed never exists unencrypted outside the setup flow.

How it works
"passkey PRF" → HKDF-SHA256 → AES-256-GCM key
AES key encrypts "Ed25519 seed" → ciphertext + IV
