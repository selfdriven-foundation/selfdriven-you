Guide · Self-testing in Australia

Test yourself, then know what it means.

You can order DNA, stool and blood tests in Australia without seeing a doctor first. Some are free and well proven. Others are expensive and tell you less than they promise. This guide sorts them, starting with what's free.

The rules · how self-testing works here

You collect the sample. An accredited lab runs the test.

Since 2010, Australia has banned the sale of home tests that you run and read yourself for serious diseases. That covers cancer and genetic markers of disease, and a 2020 review kept both banned.[10][11] What you can buy are collection kits and request forms: you provide the sample, and a lab does the testing and reporting.

Route one

Direct-access pathology

Services such as i-screen and iMedical issue the pathology request form for you. You take it to a collection centre for blood, or use a home kit for a swab, stool or breath.[1][33]

Route two

Clinician-led home kits

Some services put a health professional between you and the lab. Eugene's genetic tests include a genetic counsellor, and Microba's gut tests now go through a practitioner.[19][25]

Why overseas DNA kits don't give health reports here. In its submission to the TGA review, 23andMe said it follows the Australian ban and doesn't offer its Genetic Health Risk tests in Australia.[12]

DNA · cheek swab or saliva

Same spit, very different answers.

A cheek swab or saliva tube can tell you about ancestry, wellness traits, carrier status or inherited disease risk. The sample is the same. How much you can rely on the answer isn't.

Stool · bowel and gut

One stool test is free and proven. Most of the rest are exploratory.

A bowel screening test looks for hidden blood. A microbiome test maps the bacteria living in your gut. They answer different questions, and the evidence behind them is very different.

Strong · free
90%+of bowel cancers can be treated if found early

The National Bowel Cancer Screening Program

A free test you do at home every 2 years if you're 45–74, have a green Medicare card or are a DVA customer, and have an Australian mailing address.[7] At 45–49, request your first kit online or on 1800 627 701. From 50, a kit is mailed automatically, and the next one arrives 2 years after your last test.[8] You can also buy kits at pharmacies or online.[9]

Paid bowel test or the free kit?

i-screen's $129 Bowel Screen adds calprotectin, a marker of gut inflammation. You collect two containers from a collection centre and return your sample within 4 hours. i-screen says your GP is best placed to review the results.[6] If you're eligible for the national program, its kit does the screening job for free and arrives again every 2 years.[8]

Microba Insight is gone. Microba stopped selling its consumer Insight test in November 2024. Its current tests are designed for use with a health professional.[25]

Blood · collection centre

Start with the basics, then add one question at a time.

Usually your GP writes the pathology request. Direct-access services issue that request for you, so you can walk into a collection centre without a GP visit.[1][33]

  1. Order online

    Choose a test. i-screen needs no GP referral and no Medicare card, because it's a private service.[1][3]

  2. Get your request form by email

    Check the test page for preparation. Some blood tests need fasting. Keep taking your medicines unless the instructions say otherwise.[3]

  3. Go to a collection centre

    i-screen lists more than 1,800 affiliated, NATA-accredited centres across Australia.[1]

  4. Read your results

    Most come within about 48 hours, by email and in an online dashboard.[1] Look up any marker on Pathology Tests Explained, an independent Australian site.[31]

Anything outside the range? Take it to your GP. i-screen describes its service as wellness and education only, and says it doesn't diagnose or treat conditions.[2]

Skip IgG food intolerance tests ($365 for 96 foods at i-screen). ASCIA, the body for Australia's clinical immunology and allergy specialists, lists IgG testing to foods among unproven tests. It warns they can lead to misdiagnosis and costly, often dangerous diets. It also advises against online allergy testing.[4][29]

Prefer to go through your GP? Tests your GP orders for a clinical reason can attract a Medicare rebate, and you can choose your own pathology provider unless your doctor asks for a specific one.[32]

Before you order · six questions

Six questions to ask before you pay for any test.

These work for any provider, including ones not on this page.

  1. What will I do with the answer?

    If no result would change what you do, the test probably isn't worth it. NHMRC suggests asking this before any genetic test.[13]

  2. Is there a free or Medicare-funded route?

    Bowel screening at 45–74 and carrier screening when planning a pregnancy are both publicly funded.[8][21]

  3. Is the lab accredited?

    Look for NATA or RCPA accreditation. NATA's website lists every accredited medical testing lab in Australia.[30]

  4. Who explains the result?

    Find out who you can ask about your results, and what happens if one comes back abnormal.

  5. Where do my sample and data go?

    Overseas labs sit outside Australian privacy law, and some companies ask to use your sample for research.[13]

  6. Do I have symptoms?

    Then see your GP instead. Not sure how urgent it is? Call healthdirect on 1800 022 222, any time. In an emergency, call 000.[34]
